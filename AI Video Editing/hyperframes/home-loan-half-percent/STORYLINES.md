# Storylines (Hindi voice, 9 min, whitish hand-drawn explainer)

Source: Shiv's recording. Subject: does 0.5% more interest matter on a ₹50 lakh home loan, and what a ₹10,000 bigger EMI does to a 30-year tenure.

## 1. Two houses, one notebook  (RECOMMENDED, built)
Device: someone else's result (two customers A and B). Form: one frame that rebuilds.
A whitish notebook page. A marker pen draws two tiny houses, A and B. Everything the voice says is written or drawn next to them, when it is said: the rate, the EMI, 360 little tick marks, the interest as a stack of coins that grows while B's grows taller. The page never cuts: it scrolls, pushes in, and erases itself with a scribble when the topic changes. Second half: house B's EMI gets a +₹10,000 note stuck on it and the 30-year ruler shrinks to 15 in front of the viewer.
First two seconds: a hand draws "0.5%" in red marker, then a question mark that wobbles.
Beats: hook (0.5%?) → every loan → the promise (calculations) → two customers → EMI A vs B → total interest → the gap (₹6 lakh) → negotiate, private vs public → flexibility → the +₹10,000 EMI → 30 → 15 years → ₹38 lakh saved → close.
Look: paper #F7F4EC + grain, charcoal ink #2B2924, marker yellow #FFD84A for highlight, red #E4573D only for cost, green #3BA55D only for savings. Kalam 700 for handwriting, Baloo 2 700 for captions and big numbers.
Sound: the voice is the clock; marker squeak-free (no synth), pops on numbers landing, whoosh on page scribble-outs, one riser+impact on "₹38 lakh". No music (no catalog sign-in).

## 2. The ruler
Device: a number lands. Form: match cuts on one shape.
One shape, a 30-year ruler, holds the centre of every frame. It becomes a timeline, a stack of EMIs, a bar chart, and finally shrinks to 15. Everything else is erased around it. Bolder, more abstract, harder for a first-time viewer to follow without the two people.
First two seconds: the ruler is drawn in one stroke, "30 साल" at its end.

## 3. Bank counter
Device: a moment in someone's day. Form: object-to-object chain.
A customer at a bank counter; the officer slides a paper across; each paper becomes the next scene (the rate sheet, the EMI schedule, the prepayment form). Warm, human, but the recording is a calculation walkthrough and the papers would carry more text than the viewer can read in Hindi at speed.
