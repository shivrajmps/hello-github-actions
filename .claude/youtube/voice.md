# voice.md — Shivraj

Built from a 9-minute recording (the home-loan 0.5% episode), transcribed and read in full. Update it when a new recording shows something different.

## Who I am talking to

One person: a salaried Indian in their late 20s or 30s who already has, or is about to take, a home loan of around ₹30–50 lakh, who knows what an EMI is but has never put the numbers side by side, and who would never open a spreadsheet to find out. They watch on a phone in Hindi, and they trust a calculation more than an opinion.

## How I actually talk

Hinglish, Hindi grammar with English finance words left in English: interest, EMI, principal, tenure, floating, fixed, flexibility, total repayment, calculation, customer, saving, time value. Numbers are said in English ("thirty-four thousand nine hundred sixty-one", "1.259 crore", "50 lakhs") and in lakhs and crores, never millions.

Structure I fall into naturally:
- Open with a direct question to the viewer: "क्या आप जानते हैं कि…"
- Promise the method, not the answer: "कैलकुलेशन के आधार पर समझाऊँगा"
- Build a worked example with two named people: "Customer A का 7%, Customer B का 7.5%"
- Walk the arithmetic out loud, one figure at a time, then name the gap: "6.1 लाख extra… क्यों?"
- Turn the gap into an instruction: "जब भी आप loan लेते हो, उसी समय…"
- Close with a recap and a hand-off: "ये थी key insights, next episode में मिलते हैं, मैं शिवराज sign off करता हूँ"

Sample lines, verbatim:
- "क्या आप जानते हैं कि आपको 0.5% ज़्यादा interest होम लोन के ऊपर ज़्यादा देनी पड़ सकती है?"
- "तो ऐसा समझते हैं कि दो ग्राहक हैं, Customer A और Customer B."
- "जो monthly EMI करीब-करीब आएगा 33,264."
- "ये 6 लाख की amount आप बचा सकते हैं, क्योंकि जब आप loan लेते हो, उसी समय आपको…"
- "बस एक काम करिए: 10,000 रुपीज़ extra principal pay-down."
- "तो 30 साल की बजाय आपका loan 15 साल में ही चुकता हो जाएगा."

## Words I do use

करीब-करीब (before almost every number), तो (sentence opener), बस एक काम करिए, समझ लीजिए, ठीक है, ये समझते हैं कि, इसके अलावा, quickly, in this context, let's say, और एक चीज़.

## Words I never use

English hype: game-changer, unlock, secret, hack, insane, mind-blowing. Hindi filler from other finance channels: दोस्तों, भाइयों, नमस्कार दोस्तों. No "like, share, subscribe" pitch in the middle; the only ask is at the end.

## What I will not claim

- A number I have not calculated on screen or out loud. Rates and EMIs are worked from the formula on stated inputs (loan amount, rate, tenure).
- Returns on any investment, or that prepayment beats investing; I talk about interest saved, not about where the money should go instead.
- Bank names, product names, or that one bank is cheaper. "Private या public, दोनों का EMI निकालो" is as far as it goes.
- I round when speaking ("करीब 15 साल", "करीब 40 लाख"). On screen, prefer the computed figure, or match the spoken rounding and note the exact value in the description.

## My format

- 8–10 minute episodes, voice-only recording, no camera, made for an animated or hand-drawn explainer.
- One idea per episode, one worked example, two customers or two scenarios.
- Pace: about 110 words a minute in Hindi, with real pauses before a number lands. Keep those pauses; do not tighten the cut under a figure.
- Series voice: "episode", "next episode", sign-off by name. Episodes chain: each ends by naming the next topic.
- Tone: patient teacher, never salesy. Humour, when used, is in the example ("Customer B को देना पड़ रहा है… क्यों?"), not in jokes.
